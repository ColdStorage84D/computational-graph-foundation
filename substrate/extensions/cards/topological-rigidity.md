# Topological Rigidity

## Definition

Topological rigidity declares topological structure that should remain protected
when a subject is lifted, smoothed, compressed, embedded, simplified, or
learned.

## Parent layer

Substrate.

## Problem solved

Ordinary graph structure or metric placement may not protect higher-order
relations, cell structure, homology-like features, cycles, phase, winding, rare
regimes, or transition structure that carries domain meaning.

## Minimum substrate shape

- Graph, hypergraph, cell, simplicial, combinatorial, or other declared support
  where the protected structure appears.
- Typed field or state that carries the structure on the right support.
- Action or constraint that should preserve it.
- Validation check for collapse or loss.

## When to use

- Higher-order relations, cells, cycles, recurrence, phase, winding, or
  transition corridors matter.
- Compression or smoothing can erase important structure.
- Readout depends on preserving the protected structure.

## When not to use

- The subject has no meaningful higher-order, topological, cycle, phase, or
  recurrence structure.
- Ordinary graph and metric declarations are enough.
- The protected signal cannot be validated.

## Maturity

Research extension.

## Research boundary

This is an independently defined CGF extension. It does not derive public
support from unpublished internal physical theories, and its diagnostics do
not inherit the physical meanings of an analogous theory's flux, phase, or
charge. Any project that adopts such an analogy must keep that relationship in
Research and declare its own fields, invariants or scores, dynamics,
dimensional analysis, validation, and falsification conditions.

## Substrate-owned declarations

- Protected support: cycle, closed path, recurrent region, transition corridor,
  phase loop, regime boundary, higher-order relation, cell, filtration feature,
  or topological-domain support.
- Carrier field: an explicit nonvanishing $U(1)$-valued phase when integer
  phase degree is claimed; otherwise a unit vector, orientation, transition
  label, or other typed state with no automatic winding interpretation.
- Structure-retention term: a reference-relative action or constraint that
  penalizes declared collapse, folding, explicit-phase discontinuity, invalid
  smoothing, or loss of transition structure. A positive raw area or curvature
  quantity is not automatically a valid minimized loss.
- Topology summary: domain-defined cycle diagnostic, explicit phase degree,
  twist score, persistence-style feature, Hodge-style signal component,
  reference-relative collapse risk, or another protected-structure readout
  with its estimator, exactness status, and uncertainty declared.
- Valid scope: the graph region and time/state range where the topology claim
  is meaningful.

## Validation checks

- Unit carriers remain normalized, and any phase used for integer degree stays
  nonvanishing and explicitly $U(1)$-valued after evolution.
- Cell, boundary, or lifting declarations remain valid when higher-dimensional
  support is used.
- Exact winding is reported only for a declared phase map; orientation, twist,
  and cycle scores remain labeled as diagnostics and are stable under allowed
  perturbations.
- Persistent or Hodge-style summaries are tied to declared support when used.
- Compression or smoothing does not erase declared protected structure.
- Collapse-risk readout is tied to declared state, not hidden features.
- Boundary cases are explicit when a cycle opens, breaks, merges, or becomes
  noisy.

## Failure modes

- Treating topology as a generic feature without declaring what is protected.
- Using a topological domain because it sounds stronger than an ordinary graph.
- Preserving noise because it looks like a cycle or phase signal.
- Letting rigidity dominate the actual subject action.
- Reporting winding, homology, persistence, or twist without a validation scope.
- Using topological evidence when the carrier field is not well formed.
- Treating a generic unit vector or orientation as an integer phase winding.
- Minimizing an unsigned Gram determinant or area as an anti-collapse loss;
  doing so favors the collapse it is supposed to prevent.
- Reporting collapse risk without a declared nondegenerate reference.
